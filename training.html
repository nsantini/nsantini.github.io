📚 Exercise Video Library

Pull-Ups / Lat Pulldowns

Lats · Biceps · Core

The king of paddle strength. Mimics the exact pulling motion of paddling. If you can't do full pull-ups yet, use a band for assistance or do lat pulldowns.

Search on YouTube

Single-Arm Dumbbell Row

Lats · Rear Delt · Rhomboids

Closely mimics the paddling stroke. Keep your back flat, elbow close to the body, and drive it back deliberately. 2:1 pulling to pressing ratio is your goal.

Search on YouTube

Band External Rotation

Rotator Cuff (Infraspinatus · Teres Minor)

The most important injury-prevention exercise for surfers. Elbow at 90°, keep it glued to your side, and rotate your forearm outward against band resistance. Slow and controlled.

Search on YouTube

Face Pulls

Rear Deltoids · Rotator Cuff · Upper Traps

Pull a cable or band toward your face with elbows high and wide. Counteracts the forward-rounded posture from paddling. Do these every single session.

Search on YouTube

Band Pull-Aparts

Rear Deltoids · Rhomboids · Scapular Stabilizers

Hold a band in front of you at shoulder height, arms straight. Pull it apart until it touches your chest. Squeeze the shoulder blades at the end. Great as a warm-up too.

Search on YouTube

Prone Y-T-W Raises

Lower Traps · Posterior Shoulder · Rotator Cuff

Lie face down and raise arms into Y, T, and W shapes. Targets the lower trapezius — the muscle most commonly weak in surfers and desk workers. Use very light weight or bodyweight only.

Search on YouTube

Scapular Push-Ups

Serratus Anterior · Scapular Control

In a push-up position with arms straight, let the shoulder blades squeeze together, then push them apart by rounding the upper back. No elbow bending. Trains the "foundation" of shoulder stability.

Search on YouTube

Shoulder Dislocations

Full Shoulder Mobility · Chest Stretch

Using a band or broomstick held wide, slowly pass it overhead from front to back with straight arms. Improves overhead mobility and is an excellent warm-up before surfing or lifting.

Search on YouTube

🔥 Core Exercise Library

Side Plank with Rotation

Obliques · Glute Med · Shoulder Stability

Get into a side plank, then reach your top arm under your body and rotate back open to the sky. This is about as surf-specific as core training gets — it trains rotational power, lateral stability, and the shoulder all at once.

Search on YouTube

Mountain Climbers

Hip Flexors · Obliques · Shoulder Endurance

In a push-up position, drive alternating knees toward your chest at pace. Builds the core endurance and shoulder stability you need when paddling hard through whitewater. Keep your hips level — don't let them pike up.

Search on YouTube

Dead Bug

Deep Core · Anti-Rotation · Lower Back Protection

Lying on your back, extend opposite arm and leg toward the floor while keeping your lower back pressed flat. One of the best exercises for building the deep core stability that protects your spine during pop-ups and heavy wipeouts.

Search on YouTube

Bird Dog

Erector Spinae · Glutes · Anti-Rotation Core

On all fours, extend opposite arm and leg while keeping your hips square and back flat. Trains the back-body strength and balance you use constantly when arching on the board and during the pop-up. Slow and controlled is the key.

Search on YouTube

Hollow Body Hold

Rectus Abdominis · Hip Flexors · Full-Body Tension

Lie on your back, press your lower back into the floor, and lift your arms, shoulders, and legs off the ground into a banana shape. Builds the total-body tension and compression strength that makes your pop-up explosive and controlled.

Search on YouTube

Russian Twists

Obliques · Rotational Power · Hip Flexors

Seated with feet raised, rotate a weight or your hands side to side. Directly trains the rotational power you use when carving and pumping down the line. Add a medicine ball or plate to progress over time.

Search on YouTube

Plank to Down-Dog

Serratus Anterior · Core · Shoulder Mobility

From a plank, push your hips up into a downward dog position by pressing through your hands, then return. This hybrid move trains the serratus anterior and core together — exactly the muscles that link your shoulder stability to your paddle power.

Search on YouTube

Pallof Press

Anti-Rotation Core · Obliques · Hip Stability

Stand side-on to a cable or anchored band. Hold it at your chest, then press it straight out and hold for a second before returning. Your core has to resist rotating toward the band — this trains exactly the anti-rotation stiffness surfers need in big surf and heavy duck dives.

Search on YouTube

🧘 Shoulder Stretching Library

Hold all stretches for 30–45 seconds · Never stretch into pain · Best done when muscles are warm

🌅 Pre-surf warm-up 🌊 Post-surf / post-workout 📅 Daily (anytime)

Cross-Body Shoulder Stretch

Posterior Capsule · Rear Deltoid ⏱ 30–45s each side · Daily

Pull one arm across your chest with the opposite hand, keeping the shoulder down and relaxed. Essential for releasing tightness in the back of the shoulder that builds up from paddling.

Search on YouTube

Doorway / Pec Minor Stretch

Pectoralis Minor · Anterior Shoulder ⏱ 30–45s each side · Daily

Place your forearm on a door frame at 90° and gently lean forward. Counteracts the forward-rounded posture from hours of paddling. Do this every day — it's the single most important stretch for surfers.

Search on YouTube

Sleeper Stretch

Posterior Rotator Cuff · Internal Rotation ⏱ 30–45s each side · Post-surf/workout

Lie on your side with your arm at 90°, then gently use your other hand to press your forearm down toward the floor. Targets the posterior capsule — tightness here is a leading cause of shoulder impingement in paddlers.

Search on YouTube

Thread the Needle

Thoracic Spine · Upper Back · Posterior Shoulder ⏱ 30s each side · Pre-surf & daily

On all fours, slide one arm under your body along the floor, allowing your upper back to rotate. Brilliant for releasing thoracic spine stiffness — if your upper back is locked up, your shoulders have to overwork when paddling.

Search on YouTube

Overhead Tricep / Lat Stretch

Latissimus Dorsi · Triceps · Side Body ⏱ 30–45s each side · Post-surf/workout

Reach one arm overhead, bend it behind your head, and use the other hand to gently pull the elbow. Stretches the lats — your primary paddle muscles — which shorten and tighten with heavy use and need regular lengthening.

Search on YouTube

Child's Pose with Arm Reach

Lats · Teres Major · Thoracic Spine ⏱ 45–60s · Post-surf/workout

From child's pose, walk your hands forward and to one side to target the lat on each side. A deeply relaxing decompression for the entire shoulder complex after a hard session in the water.

Search on YouTube

Eagle Arms Stretch

Rear Deltoid · Rhomboids · Upper Traps ⏱ 30s each side · Pre-surf warm-up & daily

Cross your arms at the elbows in front of you and wrap them so the backs of the hands face each other or palms meet. Lift the elbows and pull forward. Opens up the entire posterior shoulder region beautifully.

Search on YouTube

Wall Angel Stretch

Chest · Anterior Shoulder · Thoracic Extension ⏱ 10 slow reps · Daily

Stand with your back against a wall, arms in a goalpost position. Slowly slide your arms up and down the wall, keeping your back, head, and arms in contact throughout. Reverses the "surf slump" posture and resets your shoulder alignment.

💡 Surfer's Shoulder Tips

  • Warm up before every surf: Do shoulder circles, arm swings, and 10 band pull-aparts before hitting the water.
  • 2:1 pulling to pressing: Surfers already push a lot (paddling, pop-up). Do twice as many rows/pulls as push exercises in your gym work.
  • Don't skip the rear shoulder: Impingement and rotator cuff tears happen because the front of the shoulder is overdeveloped vs. the back. Prioritize face pulls and external rotations.
  • Stretch your chest daily: Stand in a doorway and stretch the pec minor. Rounded shoulders from paddling compress the rotator cuff tendons — opening your chest counteracts this.
  • After flat spells — take it easy: Most shoulder injuries happen when surfers go from 0 to 4-hour sessions after time off. Ease back in gradually.
  • Pain = stop: These exercises should feel like effort, not pain. If anything causes shoulder pain during or after, reduce load or see a physiotherapist.
  • Stretch timing matters: Do dynamic stretches (shoulder dislocations, thread the needle) before surfing. Save deep static holds (sleeper stretch, doorway stretch) for after — when muscles are warm and pliable.
  • The daily non-negotiables: Even on rest days, do the doorway pec stretch, cross-body stretch, and wall angels. Just 5 minutes a day makes a bigger difference than any single gym session.
